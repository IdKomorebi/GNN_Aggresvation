\subsection{Comparison of Field-Level Risk Scores}\label{sec:res-def}

The mechanism case (Section~\ref{sec:res-mech}) already shows how each family of scores fails on a
known structure; a purely synthetic example with the same three failure modes---synergy, redundancy
and indirect contribution---is given in Appendix~\ref{app:toy}. Here we compare the scores as rankings of the
fields of the ten real targets, against critical fields defined by retrained ground truth.

\paragraph{Ranking}
Table~\ref{tab:ranking} compares the scores as rankings. At $\tau=0.7$, $\hat M^{(2)}$, its certified
version and the exact $M^{(2)}$ share the highest R-precision (0.894, against 0.866 for mutual information, 0.830 for the
inference graph, 0.803 for single-field retraining, 0.769 for SAGE and 0.727 for LOCO), and withholding fields in
the order of certified $M^{(2)}$ exceeds the optimum by only 0.3 fields (exact $M^{(2)}$: 0.4). At $\tau=0.5$
almost every field is critical and every score reaches an R-precision of about 0.98; here
single-field retraining ranks as well as $M^{(2)}$. As a \emph{ranking}, single-field risk is thus a strong
baseline when most fields are dangerous; the advantage of $\Mik$ lies in the decisions it supports and at high
thresholds. Attribution scores computed on one fitted
model (LOCO, permutation importance, SAGE) are the weakest throughout, because they measure what
that model uses rather than what an adversary can infer.
