\subsection{Using the Evidence in Release Review}\label{sec:application}
%===============================================================================

A release review needs both a measurement and an action that can be traced to the measured combinations. We evaluate critical-field flags and two withholding strategies under the same field roles and thresholds. The comparison quantifies the consequences of accounting for background information.

\paragraph{Critical fields}
Given $\tau$, Theorem~\ref{thm:mus} turns the risk table into a flag: field $i$ is critical if some
background of at most $K$ fields is safe on its own and becomes unsafe when $i$ is added. Over the ten
targets the single-field rule $\Vy(\{i\})>\tau$ recovers only 13\% ($\tau=0.5$) and 17\% ($\tau=0.7$) of
the critical fields. Scanning with the amortized attacker and certifying at most three candidate
backgrounds per field by retraining recovers 76--91\% of them with no false positives
(Table~\ref{tab:decision}).

\paragraph{Withholding}
The minimal unsafe sets identify which fields to withhold. Withholding every field that is unsafe
alone leaves 98\% ($\tau=0.5$) and 96\% ($\tau=0.7$) of the minimal unsafe sets of size $\le3$ intact.
Two strategies that choose fields from estimates alone, without certification---a minimum hitting set of the estimated
minimal unsafe sets, and a greedy rule that withholds the field with the largest $\hat M^{(2)}$ and
recomputes $\hat M^{(2)}$ over the remaining fields---leave 0.3--1.6\% intact while withholding at most
1.9 fields more than the ground-truth optimum.

\paragraph{Budget and review scope}
These numbers depend on the threshold, which is a policy choice: at $\tau=0.5$ almost every field is
critical in our data, at $\tau=0.9$ very few are, and the share of dangerous combinations that
single-field grading leaves intact varies from 96\% to 38\% across $\tau\in[0.5,0.9]$. They also depend
on $K$: minimal unsafe sets of size four exist that a withholding set computed at $K=2$ does not break,
and breaking them requires on average 11.3 instead of 9.4 withheld fields at $\tau=0.5$ and 6.3 instead
of 5.1 at $\tau=0.7$ (Section~\ref{sec:res-robust}). A release decision on a concrete set $A$ can also be checked directly by
evaluating $\Vy(A)$. Withholding is therefore an application of the risk profile rather than its purpose: it shows that
the per-field quantity carries enough structure---witnesses and minimal unsafe sets---to act on.
